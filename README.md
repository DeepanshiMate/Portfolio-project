## Stage 2 — AI-Powered SEO Content Production Research

### Topic I Chose
AI-Powered SEO Content Production — I chose this topic because it sits at the intersection of two fast-moving areas: artificial intelligence and search engine optimization. Understanding how AI is changing the way content is produced, distributed, and discovered felt like the most relevant and future-proof topic to research deeply.

### How I Selected Experts
I looked for practitioners who share real results, real data, and real frameworks — not just opinions. I avoided people who only talk about theory and prioritized those who work directly with clients or run their own campaigns. I also aimed for a mix of individual consultants, agency founders, and researchers to get different angles on the same topic.

### What I Collected
For each of the 10 experts I collected:
- 3 LinkedIn posts covering their most relevant and high-signal content on AI SEO
- YouTube video transcripts covering their key frameworks and strategies

All research is organized in the `/research` folder:
- `/research/sources.md` — master list of all experts with links and annotations
- `/research/linkedin-posts/` — one file per expert with their LinkedIn posts
- `/research/youtube-transcripts/` — one file per expert with key transcript insights

### Key Insights From the Research

**1. GEO and AEO are really just SEO evolving**
Multiple experts — including Lily Ray citing Google's own Danny Sullivan — confirmed that optimizing for generative AI search is really just good SEO. The core principles of content quality, authority, and relevance still apply. What has changed is how results are presented and how users consume them.

**2. Editorial media makes up 61% of AI generated responses**
Matt Diggity's research via Profound showed that AI tools pull more from editorial media than from company websites. This means Digital PR — earning press coverage and editorial mentions — has become a core part of AI SEO strategy, not just a nice-to-have.

**3. Traditional SEO powers AI search through RAG**
Lily Ray explained that LLMs use Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) to fetch fresh information from search engines like Google and Bing before generating answers. Every URL in an LLM output comes from a search engine API. This means ranking in Google is still the foundation of AI visibility.

**4. Reddit dominates LLM citations**
Ross Simmonds shared data showing Reddit has 5x the citations in ChatGPT compared to other domains and is the most cited domain on Perplexity at 46%. Brands that ignore Reddit are missing the channel that LLMs trust and cite most heavily.

**5. Speed is a competitive advantage**
Nathan Gotch demonstrated ranking #1 on Google AND getting cited in AI Overviews within 6 hours by moving fast on an emerging keyword. In a world where AI amplifies first mover advantage, speed of content production matters more than ever.

**6. AI traffic converts better but is still small**
Kevin Indig's client data showed that despite significant traffic declines from Google, revenue is relatively flat — because AI-referred traffic converts at a much higher rate. LLM traffic is still only 1-2% of total traffic but growing fast.

**7. Mass AI content generation is dangerous**
Sam Dunning and Nathan Gotch both warned about the risks of generating hundreds of pages with AI without human review. Several large SaaS companies have been hit with Google penalties for low-value AI content. The right approach is AI-assisted with human oversight — not fully automated.

**8. Consistency of brand messaging across all sources is critical**
James Dooley's research showed that if different sources say different things about your brand, AI training data gets confused and ingests your brand incorrectly. Once ingested incorrectly, it's very hard to change the AI's perception. Consistent messaging across all platforms is essential.
