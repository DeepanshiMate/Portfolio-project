## Post 2
**Topic:** Google Sheets Hack — Converting SEO Keywords into AI Prompts at Scale

A practical workflow for converting SEO keywords into AI prompts directly in Google Sheets using the =AI or =Gemini function.

The approach:
- Import keywords with real search volume from tools like Ahrefs, Semrush, or Similarweb
- Use the =AI function with the prompt below to convert keywords into natural language queries
- This allows keyword research to feed directly into AI visibility strategy at scale

Prompt used:
=AI("You are converting a terse SEO search keyword into the natural-language query a real person would actually type or speak to an AI assistant like ChatGPT, Gemini, or a voice assistant. Rewrite the keyword as a single conversational question or request that preserves the exact search intent and intent type (informational, commercial, transactional, or local). Phrase it the way someone would genuinely ask out loud in a full sentence — not in keyword shorthand. Keep any brand, product, or location named in the keyword, but do NOT invent specifics, constraints, or details that aren't already implied. Do not answer the query. Return only the rewritten prompt as plain text — no quotation marks, no preamble, no explanation, no trailing punctuation beyond a question mark. Keyword:", A2)

## Post 3
**Topic:** Brands Penalized by Google for AI Content Scaling

Three well-known brands listed as case studies of AI content scaling tools were hit hard by Google's January 20 update, with declines continuing through the most recent core update.

Key findings:
- These brands scaled page templates that manipulate AI answers — excessive "best X" content, self-promotional listicles, scaled comparison and alternative pages, and spammy structured data
- Even though AI content generation appeared isolated to one subfolder, the penalty impacted their entire domains
- All three brands are now at their lowest organic search visibility in 5+ years
- Data sourced from SISTRIX showing US organic Google visibility across full domains

Her conclusion: Google is making new changes related to "inauthentic mentions" — this is an active area of research she is continuing to investigate.
