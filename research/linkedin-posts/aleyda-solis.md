## Post 2
**Topic:** How to Build a Representative AI Search Prompt Library

One of the biggest mistakes in AI search tracking is using a non-representative prompt library to assess a brand's AI search visibility. This can give you an inaccurate view of your AI search presence.

A representative AI search prompt library should not be a random list of prompts or a tool's default prompt set. It should be a structured sample of the AI-assisted journeys that matter to your business, covering the right products, audiences, markets, journey stages, competitors and buyer constraints.

The goal is to measure the right prompts well enough to identify patterns, diagnose gaps and guide optimization actions.

This guide covers:
- What an AI search prompt library is
- The biggest mistakes to avoid
- Why representative doesn't mean exhaustive
- How to define what the prompt library needs to represent
- How to build, group and localize prompts
- How to measure AI search visibility defensibly
- How to turn prompt findings into optimization actions
- What a final prompt library can look like

## Post 3
**Topic:** AI Systems Crediting Brand Trademarks to Wrong Companies

Study findings on how AI tools mishandle brand information:

- Anthropic's Claude credited four brand trademarks to rival companies, with the error appearing in the first sentence of responses
- OpenAI's GPT-4o described brand products as generic categories and omitted the brand name in 74.2% of responses
- Google AI Overview fabricated a promotional expiration date, stripped supporting laboratory methodology from claims, and inverted a coverage exclusion

This highlights a major risk for brands relying on AI search visibility. AI tools can actively mislead users about who owns what product or trademark.
