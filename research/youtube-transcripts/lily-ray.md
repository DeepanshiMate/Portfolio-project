## Key Insights from Transcript

### 1. GEO, AEO, LLMO — It's Really Just SEO Evolving
The SEO industry has created many new acronyms — GEO (Generative Engine Optimization), AEO (Answer Engine Optimization), LLMO (Large Language Model Optimization) — but Google itself has confirmed that good SEO is really the same thing as good GEO. The core tactics haven't fundamentally changed — what's changed is how results are presented and delivered.

Key quote from Danny Sullivan (Google): "Good SEO is really the same thing as good GEO."

### 2. Traditional SEO Powers AI Search Through RAG
Large language models use Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) — a technique that allows AI tools to access and retrieve information from external knowledge sources like search engines before generating a response.

Critical insight: Every single URL that appears in an LLM output actually comes from a search engine API — Google or Bing. This means SEO has never been more important than it is now. To appear in LLMs, you need to appear in search engines first.

GPT-5 is even more reliant on search than previous models. Without grounding or RAG, it is virtually useless as a standalone tool.

### 3. Supposedly New GEO Tactics Are Rooted in Traditional SEO
Many "new" GEO tactics are actually things SEOs have been doing for years:

- Content chunking — Google's BERT model in 2019 already broke content into passages for better understanding
- Clear concise answers — FAQs, Q&As, HowTo schema have been best practice since 2011
- Multimodal content — SEOs have been producing video, audio, and image content for years
- Brand mentions and citations — digital PR and online reputation management have always been part of SEO
- Query fanout — People Also Ask and long-tail keyword optimization already addressed this

Key insight: Chunk optimization isn't really an SEO tactic — it's an AI engineering function. LLMs don't see your formatting — they see tokens. You can't control how they chunk your content. What you CAN control is creating clear, self-contained, atomic answers that stand on their own for both humans and AI.

### 4. AI Search Traffic is Still Tiny Compared to Google
Real data from Similarweb and AMSIV client data:
- LLM traffic accounts for only 1-2% of overall traffic compared to organic search at 30-50%
- Google's market share has NOT been cut into by AI tools — if anything it has grown
- ChatGPT users are actually using Google MORE after adopting ChatGPT (10.5 to 12.6 sessions per week)
- AI search is "expansionary" — an additive tool on top of Google, not a replacement

### 5. New KPIs for AI Search Era
While the tactics are similar, the metrics ARE genuinely new:
- Branded impressions — how frequently people search for and see your brand name
- Share of voice in AI search
- Direct and organic clicks to homepage from LLM recommendations
- AI search visibility by topic
- Traffic, engagement, and conversions from AI search traffic
- Visibility comparison across different LLMs

### 6. Practical Tactics for AI Search Visibility
- Answer all branded questions directly on your website using clear, concise language
- Build and expand pages for E-E-A-T — company info, leadership, products, use cases, comparison pages
- Create personalized, regionally appropriate content as LLMs become increasingly personalized
- Include FAQs, Q&As, and authentic user-generated content — LLMs cite real human conversations
- Use LLM tracking tools like Profound and Peak to monitor which domains and pages are being cited in your niche
- Keep content fresh and genuinely updated — recency matters for LLM citations
- Be active on Reddit authentically — LLMs heavily cite Reddit, Wikipedia, and YouTube
- Produce YouTube video content — all major LLMs cite YouTube content

### 7. LLM Citations Are Highly Volatile By Design
If you want your content cited in AI search, recency matters. However artificially refreshing content without making substantial changes can actually get your content demoted — both in search and in LLMs. Updates must be genuine and reflect truly new information.
