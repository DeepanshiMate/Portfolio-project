## Key Insights from Transcript

### 1. First Step — Know Where Your Audience Lives in AI Search
Different audiences use different AI tools. For most niches it's ChatGPT. For B2B and SaaS specifically, Claude is currently dominant.

Start by identifying which AI model your audience uses most, then reverse engineer how that specific model searches for information in your niche.

### 2. Each AI Model Searches Differently — Query Behavior Varies
ChatGPT tends to do very long, formal queries extracted from training data. Example: "best CRM for small business accountants HubSpot versus Trello" — 16-18 word queries.

Claude tends to keep queries between 1-6 words for the initial search, then refines with additional searches based on what comes back from the first query.

Key insight: Understanding how each AI model forms its search queries lets you reverse engineer what content to create and where to place it.

### 3. Grounding — How AI Models Find Fresh Information
Grounding is when an AI goes and searches for additional information rather than relying on its training data:
- Gemini grounds through Google
- ChatGPT grounds through Bing
- Claude uses a mixture of its own index and third party sources including Google

Almost all AI models now force grounded searches for fresh queries or current events to ensure up-to-date factual information.

Key implication: Being indexed and ranking in Google and Bing is still essential for AI visibility — AI tools are actively searching these engines in real time.

### 4. Rules for Listicles and AI Visibility
Listicles and comparison content are the most important content type for getting cited in AI search. But there are rules:
- Cannot be purely self-promotional
- Must include some drawbacks and honest assessments
- Must have some level of balance — not just saying your product is the best with no competition
- Claude actually does additional search queries to validate facts and findings

### 5. How to Get Into AI Training Data
Getting into an AI model's training data (not just grounded search) requires volume and consistency across multiple sources:
- AI companies still use Common Crawl, Internet Archive, PDF sites like Scribd, and similar sources
- Get your content, PDFs, and brand context across all overlapping file sources where AI companies download training data
- One PDF with one sentence about your brand is not enough — you need 100 pages, 10 PDFs, an entire website, guest posts, podcasts, and more

Critical warning: If all your sources say different things about your brand or entity, the training data gets confused. It will ingest your brand in the wrong way — and once ingested incorrectly, it's very hard to change the AI's opinion about your business.

Key rule: Consensus is everything. All sources must reinforce the same sentiment, the same facts, and the same positioning about your entity.

### 6. Video Content and AI Visibility
Gemini can access YouTube video transcripts even when they're not publicly available — through a backend YouTube API. So for Gemini visibility, YouTube video is extremely valuable.

For other models (ChatGPT and Claude), video prioritization depends on the query type.

Broader insight: AI models are no longer just LLMs (text only) — they are multimodal and can process text, images, video, and audio. Position your content across ALL formats — text, audio, video, and imagery — to build your entity understanding and consensus across all verticals.

### 7. Query Fanout and Reputation Dimensions
To cover query fanout effectively, brands should proactively build consensus across reputation dimensions on third party sources:
- Awards and recognition
- Case studies
- Testimonials
- Reviews

Getting these on third party sites (not just your own website) is critical because AI models use grounding to validate claims from multiple independent sources.
